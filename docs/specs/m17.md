# M17 · Sea war and reach (T15)

**Planned on 2026-11-22 against `df047ac`.** FLOW stages 1 and 2 under NR-036. M15 supplies the Shipyard and M13 supplies the war and army rules. The parked M16b technology buyer is independent.

## 1. Goal

- Build a warship at the Capital and use it to bring the field army from a home port to Sundmark, Lorran or Pellis.
- Give the defender a turn to see the landing site and a chance to intercept the crossing or close the port.
- Let the player deploy an army within home territory by rail, then see who fought, who controls the province and what its port can supply.

**Done when implemented.** From a normal seeded continent start, the player can fund a ship, declare war, land on Sundmark, win the normal land battle and see the island become a colony in the app. A defended coast can turn a landing back. The existing score, campaign, Council and replay checks below pass on their original inputs.

### Rules shared by the lanes

- **Ports and approaches.** Name an approach for each (coastal province, connected ocean) pair. Use the map’s hex neighbours and ocean tiles, excluding rivers and land. Each completed port uses the ocean at its shore. The capital’s home berth uses the nearest shore in its own province, including the capital tile if coastal. Resolve equal outlets by lowest ocean coordinate (row, then column). Bind the berth or port to that outlet for embarkation and blockade. Track the same water component when a squadron sails onward. A province adjoining disconnected seas cannot carry ships between them. An owned landing site receives troops without creating a freight port. Ordinary land adjacency stays as it is.
- **Phases and privacy.** Validate the actor’s proposed Orders against its own commitments and public resolved information. At End Turn resolve diplomacy, then settle fleet positions and any opposing contact once before dispatching armies into combat. Assess closure from those positions for both embarkation and the later connectivity/logistics phase. An isolated port loses its sea connection. A rail path can still reach it as a domestic collection hub. A blockade does not stop a defending warship sailing out from its berth to contest the coast. Equal forces can stay in contact; emit another report only on new contact or a changed outcome. Preserve the current ordering of trade settlement, deliveries, commissioned ships and new freight cars. Do not read private rival Orders to predict whether relief will succeed.
- **Fleet and army ownership.** Use one authoritative operational hull record in `GameState.fleets` and keep nation fleet IDs consistent. Berth and approach are distinct locations. A commissioned squadron must be on the sea that connects an embarkation port to the target for FR-550. Ships can sail to a neutral coast before war. A hostile landing-site order requires a preceding resolved arrival and an active war. Standing at a port’s approach is public after arrival and can close the port immediately. The previous resolved site must still support the same target at dispatch. Rechecking diplomatic ownership after a conquest prevents an enemy assault silently turning into friendly reinforcement. On capital fall, retire the fallen power’s fleet, pending missions and blockades with its normal defeat cleanup.
- **Orders and saves.** Start with the existing set-count Shipyard contract and editable, per-regiment army intents. One regiment can have only one move, disband or attack intent. Validation failure leaves the previous legal plan and its reservation intact. Treat naval and rail plans as ordinary serializable Orders. Derive remaining capacity from effective intents. Use pure functions of state and context throughout. Preserve seeded land combat, historical replay in `store/sessionReplay.ts` and optional-field defaults at the save boundary.
- **Evidence before balance.** Keep the original frozen policies, seeds, goals and golden hashes. In the new report, compare the same idle and diplomat seeds 1–24 and a legal naval exercise with a defender and an attacker. Record build/arrival/station/first legal landing turns, attempts/successes per eligible opportunity, hull commitments, ordinary cargo used, treasury, payroll, province captures and Council votes. Report absent opportunities explicitly. The original-game play-tests on the cards supply the comparison for future tuning.

## 5. Owner decision cards

On 2026-11-22 the owner approved option (a) on all three cards. Original-game comparisons are follow-up play-tests, independent of whether an M17 test passes.

#### Q144. How much water can a squadron cross in one turn?

The small fixed map has ocean hexes and coastal provinces, but no historical sea-zone boundaries. Rail deployment also needs a land/sea boundary.

- (a) **Use province approaches.** A reachable coast takes one sail Order, followed by a separate station turn. Domestic rail stays on the same landmass. A stationed fleet can also reinforce a friendly shore within its support limit.
- (b) **Map several sea zones.** Ships travel by their printed speed across a new zone map. The crossing may take more turns.

Suggestion: Use province approaches for the first playable crossing.

Objection: Short and long voyages take the same time, and holding one approach can close several ports in that province. The original’s limit for follow-up reinforcements is uncertain.

Play-test: Trace a short and a long voyage in Imperialism and record zones, travel turns, landing-site turn and when rail can cross to a colony.

#### Q145. What happens when an enemy fleet intercepts a landing?

Owner answer, 2026-11-22: "as any tile, when two enemies meet on the same tile (landing or not landing) there is a battle between the two." Two allies landing on one target are allowed: the stronger attacks first, the other goes second only if the first failed; otherwise its attack is cancelled, since it cannot attack an ally.

- Decided: **a real sea battle** (FR-552) with ship losses, and **ordered allied attacks** (FR-560).
- Default taken for the unknown numbers (rules gap D3: damage per shot and hit chance are not documented): 3 rounds, longer range fires first, 50% hit chance, damage = firepower − armour (at least 1), repair 25% of hull per turn at berth. Each is one named constant.
- Objection: these numbers are ours, not the original's. Battle speed is unused.
- Note: in this remake the landing and its land battle happen in the same End Turn, one turn after the landing site is set up (Q144), so "attack on the next turn" means the turn after the site is set up.

Play-test (owner plans to install the original): record hull, range, firepower, armour, damage per round and losses across repeated matched sea battles, and how two allied landings on one province resolve.

#### Q146. When should the computer spend on warships?

M15 buys merchant holds from observed cargo use. Previous speculative spending weakened Council votes and the one-province campaign. The original naval budget and invasion timing are unknown.

- (a) **Respond to a visible threat.** Use existing hulls or fund one Frigate after a hostile squadron is seen off one of the computer’s ports.
- (b) **Prepare an offensive fleet.** Divert stocks before a threat to seek island wars. This also changes the opening army and diplomacy.

Suggestion: Respond to a visible threat. Measure offensive opportunities before choosing a purchase or invasion policy.

Owner answer, 2026-11-22: this rule is the "Easy" level. Harder levels need a real naval strategy: build a fleet in advance according to the nation's position, prepare the attack fleet a few turns before a planned war, and prepare the defence when another power is flagged as aggressive. Recorded in `docs/specs/war-and-difficulty-notes.md`; it needs the DifficultyProfile spec first.

Objection: A quiet player can take an island minor without a rival naval race, and a reactive build may arrive too late to save a port.

Play-test: Play comparable quiet and threatened openings in the original. Record first hull, launches, target coast, available stores, first landing and the army or industry order delayed.

## 7. Risks and later work

| Risk | What the implementation must demonstrate |
|---|---|
| A declaration becomes possible without troops ever reaching the target | Follow the normal funding and embarkation path on the stock map. Cover both validation and resolution, including a changed port, peace and lost support. |

| A fleet stationed offshore discloses the defender’s current order | UI and AI see only resolved foreign positions. Use a private-order swap with an active public threat, so a privacy check can detect a leak. |
| A capture appears to create a working port | M9 removes captured infrastructure. Check physical control, Council ownership and actual freight collection separately. A beachhead carries regiments only. |
