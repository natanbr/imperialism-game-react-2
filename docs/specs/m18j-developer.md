# M18j — The Developer and overseas profit

Spec only · 2026-11-26 · `claude/m18j-developer` · Design base `6eedc75` (M18i merged in PR #57)

## Rules summary

The Developer is the eighth civilian. It is a diplomatic reward, never built, and a Great Power has at most one at a time. It may work only in Minor Nations where its nation holds an Embassy. There it buys land for 20 × the commodity's last market price: forests, fertile or barren hills, mountains, cotton plantations, deserts, tundra and swamps, never food tiles. A flag in the buyer's colour marks the tile, and no other Great Power may use it. A non-mineral tile produces at level 0 at once. Minerals and oil produce nothing until a Miner or Driller works them. The Minor Nation sells the output on the world market and pays the buyer a share, which rises with relations and is 100% in a colony. The Deal Book shows it as Overseas Profits. Gold and gems profits need no merchant ships. A buyer who buys its own output pays full price and receives the share back. A conqueror owns tiles that others developed. Colonisation is said to wipe other powers' investments, and a colony boycott removes them. [Civilians §5](../rules/03-civilian-units.md#5-developer) · [Minor Nations and colonies §§3–4, 7.1](../rules/07-diplomacy-and-minors.md) · [Prices §4](../rules/06-trade-and-market.md#4-prices) · [Gaps C3, F15, H15](../rules/gaps.md)

**Conflicting overseas-share evidence (F15).** Ledger sums give about 75% to a partner. In one example a wool unit sold at $126 paid $95 after a pact. They give 100% in a colony: cotton from Qak paid 3 units × $75. The manual also says colonies pay 100%. The 1998 economics guide gives "royalty 30% in a partner and 12% in a colony". The rules files call that guide ambiguous. One reading reconciles the sources, though nobody has checked it against the guide's text: the royalty is the Minor Nation's cut. The buyer would then keep 70% and 88%, which is close to the ledger's 75% and 100%. The only break-even report also supports a high share. It says about 20 turns at 20 × price per tile. One unit a turn at 75% would take 27 turns, and at 30% it would take 67 turns. This spec uses 75% at the unlock level and 100% in a colony (the default already recorded in gaps F15). The curve between them is inferred.

**What today's maps allow.** On the continent and the random maps the Minor Nations hold timber, coal, iron ore and cotton, plus horses and food tiles, which cannot be bought. They hold no wool, gold, gems or oil, so those four are tested on hand-built states only. Random-map Minor Nations keep some coal and iron seams hidden. Buying one would first need a Prospector in Minor Nation land, which this package does not add. Every remake colony comes from conquest (FR-224), and conquest passes the colony's land to the conqueror. A colony whose land still belongs to the Minor Nation needs Join the Empire or intervention (card Q37), and neither is built. Two islands has no Minor Nations, so no rule in this package can run there. ## Where the rule fits

- **The unit and the naming conflict.** `WorkerType.Developer` exists today as a generic improver. `DEVELOPMENT_TERRAINS` has no entry for it, so `checkDevelopment` lets it raise any terrain. No path creates one: `START_CIVILIANS` and `FIELD_WORKERS` list seven types without it, and `UniversityCivilian` excludes it. The art and the status text already exist. Retire the improver behaviour and keep the member, because the game has never written the value to a save. ## Open questions and inferred defaults

Every default below is **inferred, owner to confirm**. The build uses it until the owner decides.

| Decision for the owner | Inferred default for the build |
|---|---|
| **Overseas share for a partner and a colony (F15).** Ledger 75% / 100% against the 1998 guide's royalty of 30% / 12% | 75% at the unlock level and 100% in a colony, following the ledger, the manual and the break-even report. The guide's figures probably describe the Minor Nation's cut (70% / 88% to the holder), but nobody has checked that against its text. |
| **The share curve across levels** ("up to 100% as relations improve") | Levels 0–8 pay 15, 25, 35, 45, 55, 65, 75, 90 and 100%; the holder's colony pays 100%. The level is read at the trade phase. Nothing records a share level by level (gap H15). |
| **Unlock threshold** (seen at about "level 7 of 9"; the manual's sequence is embassy, then pact, then years of trade) | An embassy with an independent Minor Nation and relation level 6 or above (60 points, grey) with that same Minor Nation. A pact is not required. A Developer is granted again when the nation has none. It cannot be disbanded. |
| **Price base when a commodity has no last price** | A trade commodity uses the board price, or `START_PRICES` when the market lacks one. Gold costs 20 × $200 = $4,000 and gems 20 × $500 = $10,000, from the existing cash values. |
| **What happens to tiles on conquest and colonisation** | Holdings end without refund, and the conqueror owns the tile at its current level, the holder included. Other nations' civilians go home. A fallen Great Power's holdings end. |
| **The colony boycott** | Specify the rule and build the removal helper. Defer the Order and its screen to Join the Empire (Q37), because conquest colonies already end all holdings. |
| **Is profit paid on every unit, or only on units a Great Power buys?** | Every developed unit, valued at the board price. The remake has two Great Powers where Imp1 has seven, so paying only on units they buy would often pay nothing. |
| **Does profit need merchant ships?** | None does. The gold and gems note is read as "paid as cash, never on the Board". The other reading would charge one free hold per unit after the trade deals. |
| **Level after purchase** | 0, so a mineral or oil tile needs a Miner or Driller before it yields anything. |
| **Credit** | Allowed within the credit limit, like every other civilian job. The embassy alone requires cash. |
| **Competing buyers in one turn** | The Minor Nation's partner ranking decides, and the others are refunded. |
| **Score and relations** | Holdings add no score, and purchases and profits are not trades. Profit enters the treasury and its score points. |
| **Frozen test policies** | The FR-509 diplomat refuses `buyLand`, so FR-545 keeps measuring the same diplomacy. The `scripted` policy is the live planner by definition. |
| **The computer's payback rule** | It buys only when the expected profit before the turn limit covers the price plus any level-1 job, after the existing reserves, and for minerals or oil only with an idle Miner or Driller. In 40-turn games it will rarely buy. At the 75% unlock share, a level-0 tile at about $110 pays back only if bought by about turn 14 (20 / 0.75 ≈ 27 paid turns). A coal or iron tile at about $115 plus a $100 mine pays back only if bought by about turn 24 (2 units, first paid three End Turns later). The rule matters mainly for the longer calendar (FR-593, FR-594). |
| **Prospecting in Minor Nations** (allowed by the rules with an embassy) | Not in this package. Hidden seams in random Minor Nations cannot be bought until a later package adds it. |
