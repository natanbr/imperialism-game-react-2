## M13 Computer nations that play (T11)

### 1. Goal

The computer nation becomes a real opponent on the continent, and it plays by the player's rules. Today, once it
takes Arvelia (turn 16 or 17), it does nothing for the rest of the game. When invaded it raises no troops, its
workforce shrinks from 7 to 4, it never trades with the player and it never competes for the minors' Council votes.
After M13 it does five things. It raises regiments it can actually pay and staff. When invaded it defends and
counter-attacks. It keeps its workforce and staffs its mills. It competes for the minors' votes with grants. It trades
steel, lumber and goods with the player. Once Arvelia is gone it may go to war with the player, but only for one
province, and then it offers peace. Every rule reads only what a human could see and acts only through `Order`s. Each
rule acts only on a world with provinces or in "partners", so the v1 two-islands baselines stay unchanged.

**What Nathan will see in the app:**
- Around turn 16 or 17 the news says Valdoria declared war on Arvelia and took it (as today).
- In the turns before each Council meeting the news shows "Valdoria gives Sundmark a grant of $1,000". In Diplomacy,
  Sundmark's points with Valdoria climb past 50, and at the turn-30 Council Sundmark votes for Valdoria.
- On the Board of Trade, the steel and lumber rows say "Valdoria bid for 2 last turn". Offering one sells it at End
  Turn, and the Deal Book shows "Sold 1 Steel to Valdoria".
- From about turn 20 Valdoria's army gathers next to Testland North-East. The HUD warns "Valdoria's army at our border:
  Testland North-East (52 against our 25)". Valdoria then declares war (HUD: "At war with Valdoria (since turn N)") and
  takes that one province. On the next turn the Accept Offer dialog says "Valdoria asks for an end to our war". It never
  goes for the capital.
- If the player invades Valdoria, the news shows "Valdoria recruits Cuirassiers" from the first turn of the threat.
  Its new regiments march to the threatened province, and it attacks a weakly held province back.

**Done when:** every M13 row is tested. The sim thresholds (table in FR-500) pass on seeds 1 to 24. FR-125 and the
two-islands pins pass with no re-record. The full gate is green. The QA checks below are seen in the browser.

## M14 A game that can end well (T12)

### 1. Goal

Every continent game ends with a clear result that follows the rules. The last Council is a forced vote, so the turn-40
meeting always picks a winner: the nominee with more governors (rules 01 section 4, "at the tenth meeting every governor
must choose"). A peaceful Council win becomes reachable. With only two Great Powers, 9 of 12 votes can never be reached
without taking a province from the rival: the best peaceful total is own 4 + 4 minors = 8 (AISIM-05). So M14 needs card
Q121. The final ranking counts provinces and army, and a conquered province no longer adds points for tiles nobody
collects. With M13's active rival, a passive player now loses the game, and a player who courts the minors can win
it without a war.

## Order of M14 to M16, and why

M14 first: it is the smallest (M), and it uses M13's active rival, so the game now has stakes. It also fixes the
open end-of-game lies: no winner at turn 40, a ranking that ignores provinces, conquest as the cheapest score. M15
next: it gives cash and goods a use, lifts the 4-hold cap and the 4-horse cap, and both M16 and M17 depend on it. M16
last of the three: it depends on M15 and on Q130. It is also the largest balance change and the one most likely to move
FR-125. Then M17 (sea war and reach; needs the Shipyard of M15), M15b living minors (Q128), and M18.
